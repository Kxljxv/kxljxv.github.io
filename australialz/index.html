Australia's System of Government

Australia is a federal parliamentary constitutional monarchy. The system is based on liberal democracy, with religious tolerance, freedom of speech, and the rule of law. The Australian Constitution (effective 1901) defines the framework and the separation of powers. Australia is comprised of six states and two self-governing territories, each with its own government, under a national (federal) government headquartered in Canberra.

  • Parliamentary Government: The Executive comes from within the Parliament and is responsible to it.
  • Constitutional Monarchy: The King is the Head of State, represented by the Governor-General, but acts only on advice of elected ministers.
  • Three Levels: Federal, State/Territory, and Local Government.

The Separation of Powers

The Constitution separates power into three branches to provide checks and balances:

Legislative

Makes and changes the law (Parliament: House of Representatives & Senate)

Executive

Puts laws into action (Prime Minister, Cabinet, Departments, Governor-General)

Judiciary

Interprets and applies the law (High Court & Federal Courts)
  • Why separation? Prevents oppressive government and maintains rule of law.
  • Australia's twist: Ministers must be members of Parliament, so Legislative & Executive overlap, but checks exist via Opposition and bicameral system.

Parliament, Cabinet, and the Executive

  • Bicameral Parliament: House of Representatives (Lower House) and Senate (Upper House).
  • Forming Government: Party with most seats in House of Representatives forms government. Leader becomes Prime Minister.
  • Cabinet: PM and senior ministers set government policy and major decisions.

  • Checks and balances: Opposition parties, Senate reviews, and judiciary independence.

States, Territories, and Local Government

  • States: 6 states, each with its own Constitution, Parliament (bicameral except QLD), Premier, and Governor.
  • Territories: 2 self-governing territories, each with a single-chamber Parliament.
  • Local: About 900 local councils/shire bodies, handle planning, waste, local roads, recreation, etc.
